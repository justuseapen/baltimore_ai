**Baltimore’s current AI story is about applied work and new ways into the community:** health-data infrastructure, cybersecurity workflows, regional research, and public gatherings. This field report covers developments verified by September 8, 2026, with each announcement dated and linked to its source.

## The latest developments worth knowing

### September 2: Enveil expands public-sector distribution

Fulton-connected [Enveil](/companies/enveil) [announced a Carahsoft partnership](https://www.enveil.com/enveil-partners-with-carahsoft/) for its secure data-use software. Carahsoft will act as its public-sector distributor. This is a concrete channel-development update for a regional company working on privacy-preserving AI and analytics; no contract value was disclosed in the cited announcement.

### August 5: CMS documents b.well’s live network

A [CMS showcase updated August 5](https://www.cms.gov/initiatives/health-technology-ecosystem/overview/pledge-demo-showcase/b-well) describes Baltimore-based [b.well’s](/companies/b-well-connected-health) CMS Aligned Network as live in production. That corroborates a [July 29 company announcement](https://resources.icanbwell.com/bwells-cms-aligned-network-goes-live-real-patient-data-now-exchanging-across-major-health-plans-digital-platforms-and-health-systems/). The distinction matters: a working data network is infrastructure for connected health, not proof of clinical effectiveness for AI tools built around it.

### July 14: Redshred’s COACH selected for a maritime demonstration

[Redshred](/companies/redshred) [announced selection](https://www.redshred.com/news/redshred-selected-to-demo-at-bluetide-2026-showcasing-ai-powered-procedural-guidance-for-maritime-operations) for an August 27 BlueTIDE demonstration of AI procedural guidance. The source supports the selection and planned use case. We have not treated that pre-event announcement as proof that the demonstration subsequently occurred.

### June 22: TEDCO backs EcoMap

[TEDCO announced a Seed Funds investment of more than $100,000](https://www.tedcomd.com/press-release/tedco-invests-ecomap-technologies) in Baltimore-based [EcoMap Technologies](/companies/ecomap-technologies), which develops AI resource mapping and conversational navigation. The figure is TEDCO’s investment, not a stated total funding round.

### June and July: practical AI adoption reaches local businesses

Maryland’s [July 16 Innovation Team newsletter](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/MDGOV/bulletins/41ff420) reports a June Baltimore workshop involving more than 70 small and medium businesses, organized with Anthropic, the Greater Baltimore Committee, and Tenex. It describes hands-on work with business tasks. This is evidence of recent local adoption activity, rather than an open invitation to a past workshop.

### May 28: Maryland launches an AI Innovation Lab

The state [announced an AI Innovation Lab](https://governor.maryland.gov/news/press-releases/governor-moore-launches-new-ai-innovation-lab-upscale-ai-adoption-and-experimentation) within DoIT to support state-agency prototyping, training, and security evaluation. It is a statewide government program, not a public startup workspace. Our [resource profile](/resources/maryland-ai-innovation-lab) explains that scope.

### May 20: Tenable announces work with Anthropic

Columbia-based [Tenable](/companies/tenable) [announced Claude-powered workflows](https://www.tenable.com/press-releases/tenable-partners-with-anthropic-for-ai-driven-exposure-management) for Hexa AI and its exposure-management platform. It is a concrete example of generative and agentic AI becoming part of an established regional cybersecurity business.

## The research and startup pipeline

The institute is active; the new buildings are still a future facility. ## What the ecosystem numbers do—and do not—say

UpSurge’s **2026 report describes 2025 activity**, including $559.8 million across 77 regional deals. That is overall ecosystem funding, not Baltimore AI funding or a 2026 year-to-date figure. Its geography includes Baltimore City and seven surrounding counties, and its company sample is not a census. [Read the report and methodology](https://upsurgebaltimore.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/2026-Greater-Baltimore-Ecosystem-Report.pdf).

We do not repeat its AI-deal count as a clean measure of AI activity: the report distinguishes AI-native products from products using AI, while elsewhere describing funded cybersecurity companies as AI-native. That classification ambiguity does not support a simple “Baltimore has no AI investment” conclusion.
