The Baltimore–DC corridor contains companies that use AI in security operations and companies that protect sensitive data used by AI. These are related markets with different products. Location matters too: Baltimore City, Columbia, and Fulton should not all be labeled simply “Baltimore headquarters.”

## AI for exposure management and security operations

**[Tenable](/companies/tenable), Columbia:** on May 20, 2026, the company [announced an Anthropic partnership](https://www.tenable.com/press-releases/tenable-partners-with-anthropic-for-ai-driven-exposure-management), including Claude-powered workflows in Hexa AI. This is a dated company announcement about its product direction, not independent proof of performance.

**[ZeroFox](/companies/zerofox), Baltimore:** its external cybersecurity business uses AI analytics. The [May 2024 acquisition completion](https://www.zerofox.com/press-release/haveli-completes-acquisition-of-zerofox/) also corrects older descriptions of the company as publicly traded.

**[Huntress](/companies/huntress), Columbia:** its [current platform page](https://www.huntress.com/) describes a service backed by an AI-centric security operations center. Its [contact page](https://www.huntress.com/contact-us) identifies Columbia, while its distributed workforce extends beyond the region.

## Protecting sensitive data used in AI

**[Enveil](/companies/enveil), Fulton:** the company’s [Secure AI offering](https://www.enveil.com/secureai/) covers privacy-preserving machine learning. A September 2, 2026 [Carahsoft partnership announcement](https://www.enveil.com/enveil-partners-with-carahsoft/) establishes a new public-sector distribution channel. That is a distribution milestone, not a disclosed customer count or contract value.

A privacy-preserving computation product solves a different problem from an endpoint detection platform. Compare the data flows, threat model, integrations, and deployment constraints before treating two vendors as alternatives.

## What to check when evaluating a provider

Ask what the AI actually does: drafting analysis, prioritizing alerts, recommending actions, or taking actions automatically. Request evidence on the intended deployment, not a generic benchmark. Clarify human review, failure handling, supported integrations, and what data crosses organizational boundaries.
