You can study AI in the Baltimore area through a degree, professional training, research participation, or community events. Choose the format around your goal: understanding the foundations, building a system, applying AI at work, or meeting collaborators. These paths are complementary rather than a universal ranking of schools.

## Degrees and research study

**UMBC’s MS in Artificial Intelligence:** a [June 20, 2026 department announcement](https://www.csee.umbc.edu/news-events/post/160791/) describes the program and its research, development, and deployment focus. That announcement’s August 1 deadline for Fall 2026 has passed. Use the program link on that page for the next admission cycle; do not treat an old deadline as an open application.

**Johns Hopkins Engineering for Professionals:** its [AI program page](https://ep.jhu.edu/programs/artificial-intelligence/) describes an online master’s pathway. An online program offered by a Baltimore university is not the same thing as an in-person Baltimore course. Review the current prerequisites, format, and degree requirements directly.

**Research exploration:** [UMBC’s Center for AI](https://ai.umbc.edu/) publishes lab and faculty information. [Johns Hopkins MINDS](https://www.minds.jhu.edu/) connects theoretical data science, research, and education. Read individual researchers’ work to find a substantive match before asking about participation.

## Professional training for practical work

[UMBC Training Centers](/resources/umbc-ai-training) offers professional AI learning outside the university degree programs. Its [current AI course schedule](https://www.umbctraining.com/course-schedule/?technology=ai-learning-pass) is the source for dates, delivery methods, and enrollment information. Course length, price, and prerequisites vary.

For someone introducing AI at work, a useful first project has a bounded workflow, test examples, and a way to compare the new process with the current one. A course is easier to apply when you arrive with that concrete problem.

## Founder programs and community

The [Pava Center](/resources/fastforward-u), formerly FastForward U, has distinct student, alumni, and community offerings. Consult its [program pages](https://pavacenter.jhu.edu/) for eligibility; not every program is open to everyone.

Baltimore also has current AI gatherings. The [fall events guide](/guides/baltimore-ai-events-fall-2026) includes an announced AI Collective networking night and a Johns Hopkins symposium explicitly open to the public. These verified listings replace the old assumption that the city has no practitioner community worth seeking out.

## A practical way to choose

1. Write down what you want to be able to do after learning: build a model, evaluate an AI application, automate a workflow, or understand research.
2. Check the program’s expected background, delivery format, time commitment, and current total cost.
3. Make a small project or learning plan before committing to a longer program.
4. Use a local event to ask practitioners which skills they use in the work you want to do.

We have not independently compared tuition, placement rates, or teaching quality. This guide identifies verified options and their formats, not a promise of employment or a “best value” ranking.
